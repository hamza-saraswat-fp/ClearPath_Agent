# ClearPath Test Scenario: CSS Mechanical

## Scenario Brief

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Company** | CSS Mechanical |
| **Industry** | Commercial HVAC & Refrigeration |
| **Company Size** | Small — owner/operator (Tony/Steve) + field technicians |
| **Job Types** | Service/repair calls, planned maintenance, plumbing (expanding into by April) |
| **Customers** | Commercial/industrial facilities (plants, clinics, food service — e.g., Walmart, SNP Clinic, Dawn Foods) |
| **Current Tools** | Paper work order forms, Excel for invoicing, PDF export to email customers, manual PO tracking |
| **Key Pain Points** | Techs do things differently; need a standardized process. High error rate when techs pick line items on mobile (40,000 items, wrong labor rates selected — "Kentucky government HVAC instead of boiler and they're different rates"). Receipt/parts photos need to stay internal, not go to customer. Need EPA refrigerant tracking that's searchable/reportable. Office needs to review and check everything techs submit before invoicing. |
| **Who Creates Jobs** | Technicians — 90% of the time. They get the call directly from the on-site customer and initiate the job on their phone. |
| **Dispatch Model** | Tech-initiated, not office-dispatched. Customer calls tech directly ("hey, sends me a text, can you come help me?"). Tech creates the job on-site. |
| **Assets** | Track equipment at customer sites (units with serial numbers, refrigerant history). Customers have multiple locations/sites. |
| **Compliance** | EPA refrigerant tracking — need to report how much refrigerant was used per unit over time periods (quarterly, yearly, up to 7 years). Even zero-usage months require a form for compliance. |

## Customer Narrative

> We're CSS Mechanical. We do commercial HVAC and refrigeration — service calls, planned maintenance, that kind of thing. We work with big commercial customers like Walmart, Dawn Foods, plants, clinics. Our customers have multiple locations and multiple pieces of equipment at each site that we track.
>
> Right now, here's how things work. A tech gets a phone call — 90 percent of the time it's the technician getting the call directly from the customer on site. The customer says "hey, I need you to come over and look at the SNP clinic." Or they'll send a text — "can you come help me?" The tech says "I'll pick you up" and they go.
>
> When they get to the job, they do their thing. They might need to go get parts from the supply house. We write a PO for everything we buy — that's important, we need to track every purchase order. The tech puts everything on the work order form — the time, the technician name, the date, hours worked, whether it's overtime or regular. From that, we can tell what we need to bill — like "I need to bill four regular hours and eight overtime hours."
>
> Some jobs they have the customer sign at the bottom of the form as proof of agreement. They sign and they agree, and then we have proof of that.
>
> Then they turn the paperwork in to me, and I start doing an Excel file for an invoice. I save it and print it to PDF so I can send it to the customer. That's been our process.
>
> The problem is everybody does it somewhat differently. What I want is a proper procedure to tell the guys — "you need to do it this way." When we're training these guys, I need to say: first thing you need to do is go in, create a job, pick the customer, fill that out properly. Then go in and clock into your time after you create the job.
>
> So the flow we're looking at now is: tech gets the call, creates the job on their phone. They pick the customer, add the job title — repair, service, whatever. They assign it to themselves, add the site location. Then they go do the work.
>
> While they're at the job, they take photos — pictures of the equipment, serial number tags, whatever they need. If they went and bought parts, they take a picture of the receipt and upload it to the job. But here's the thing — we don't want the customer to see what we paid for parts. Those receipt photos need to stay internal. The customer should never see our costs.
>
> Then they fill out the completion report form — that's our custom form. They put in everything: what they did, the parts they used, the time, all that. Then they get the customer's signature on it. The signature is approval of the work — like "hey, I'm in agreement with this, now send me an invoice."
>
> After that, they mark the job complete. Now it's the invoicing phase. But the tech doesn't do the invoice — that comes back to us in the office. We go to the job, create an invoice, and we have to look at that form and enter the right line items. The office has to double-check everything because techs make mistakes. They'll pick the wrong labor rate — Kentucky government HVAC instead of Kentucky government boiler, and they're different rates. So there's always a check and balance. The invoicing has got to be double-checked.
>
> One more thing — we track assets. Every piece of equipment at a customer's site needs a record. We need to track refrigerant usage per unit for EPA compliance. Customers call and say "I need to know how much refrigerant you used on this unit in the last seven years." We need that to be searchable and reportable, not just buried in a form somewhere. Even if we didn't use any refrigerant in a month — no leaks, no repairs — we still need to submit a zero-usage form. The customer still wants the report for compliance.
>
> We're also planning to expand into plumbing by April, so we'll need to handle that as a different type of work eventually.
