## Customer Narrative

> I'm Scott, I run AK Doors out of Alaska. We do garage door sales and installation — residential customers and contractors. The thing that makes us different is I don't buy locally. I order all my doors from vendors down in the States and ship them up here by freight container. That's actually my specialty — I've done this with three or four different types of businesses. Being in Alaska, it's really hard to get product, so I'm basically more of an importer than I am a garage door guy.
>
> Here's how a typical job flows. Say you're a contractor or a homeowner and you order doors from me. I'm going to give you an estimate. You approve it and say go. But then there's a whole bunch of steps before I can actually install your doors.
>
> First, I do what's called a group ship rate. I tell the customer — if your timeline can support this, I'm going to give you a group ship rate. That means I'm not ordering your door right away. I'm going to gather your order, you give me your deposit, and we hold on that until I get enough orders to fill a full container of doors. If we do that, you're paying hundreds of dollars for shipping instead of thousands.
>
> So I've got to keep track of this group that's building. Depending on when you come in, the next step after you've approved your estimate is for me to continue to build that group.
>
> Then once I have enough and I say go on it, it's four to six weeks out from that date. I have to stay on top of my vendor — if I don't follow up, they might sit on the product for a week and send it out late even though they had it.
>
> Then about a week or two before the product is ready in Seattle, I've got to buy a Connex — that's a shipping container. It takes a week to get it certified for seaworthy. Then I've got to get a freight company to put it on a chassis, drive it to the vendor, drop it off in time for them to load it. Once loaded, it ships out the following Friday. Then it's about a week of travel before it gets here.
>
> When it arrives, we unload it, inventory it, and then I can finally say "we're ready to install your door."
>
> All those steps — I need to keep track of them. It's really easy to fall behind any given week and realize "oh, I didn't order a container, shoot." So what I need is different types of project templates with different pipelines. Each type of project has its own stages.
>
> What I'm hoping for is that when we activate a workflow and say we're in the first stage, the system knows that the second stage is coming up in two weeks. It needs to move on in two weeks. And maybe there are calendar events or reminders that show up assigned to specific people. Can the workflow be time-based like that?
>
> And when I say "okay, this is this type of project," can the workflow build all that out automatically? Like select the project type, and the whole pipeline with all the dates, timelines, jobs, and site visits gets created?
>
> For the tech side of things, I want them to clock in per job. When they change their status to on site or in progress, it should prompt them to clock in. And when they clock out of a job, it should keep them clocked in for their general daily timesheet. We need both running in parallel so I can see job-specific hours and overall hours.
>
> I'm pretty tech-savvy — I've set up systems like this for multiple businesses. I'd rather do Q&A sessions where I bring specific questions than sit through standard training. Just let me build it and ask questions as I go.
