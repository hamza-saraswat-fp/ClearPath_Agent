# ClearPath Test Scenario: Barnett Heating & Cooling

## Scenario Brief

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Company** | Barnett Heating & Cooling |
| **Industry** | Residential/Commercial HVAC |
| **Company Size** | Small — owner (Lee), office staff handling admin, field technicians (service techs + installers) |
| **Job Types** | Service calls and installs — "All we do is install and service it." Two distinct workflows needed. |
| **Current Tools** | Another software program (unnamed) where they track time as "segments" for multi-day jobs. Maintenance checklists exist as PDFs they want to digitize. |
| **Key Pain Points** | Techs need to be held accountable — must take photos, get signatures, fill out forms, and clean up before they can close a job. Different checklist for service vs install. Techs sometimes create invoices in the field and collect payment on the spot (checks). Multi-day installs need time tracking even when there's no invoice on day one. |
| **Dispatch Model** | Office-dispatched. Service calls are single-visit jobs. Installs may be multi-day (site visits under one work order, not separate jobs). |
| **Signature Process** | Two-signature flow for service calls: (1) signature before starting the diagnostic — tech explains to customer what they'll be doing, customer authorizes the diagnostic. (2) Signature after diagnostic to authorize the repair. Both collected on the spot via mobile. |

## Customer Narrative

> We're Barnett Heating and Cooling. We do two things — install and service. That's it. So we'd need two different checklists, a service checklist and an install checklist.
>
> For a service call, here's what I want the techs to do every single time. First, upload pictures — before and after. They've got to get a signature from the customer before they start. When they get on the job, they've got to explain to the customer that they'll be doing a diagnostic, and they'll need a signature for them to perform the diagnostic. Then after they figure out what's wrong, they'll need a second signature from the customer to perform the actual repair.
>
> Then they need to fill out an invoice of what they've done before they leave the job. We want to make sure they pick up after themselves — the job site needs to be clean. Yes, making sure the space is clean. And then getting a final signature before they leave.
>
> I want all of these steps to be required. The guys can't mark a job as completed unless they've finished every step on the checklist. That's important.
>
> Sometimes the guys in the field create invoices themselves. They need to take that invoice in the field and get a check for it right there. So sometimes they're collecting payment on the spot.
>
> For installs, it's a little different. An install might be a two-day kind of thing — it's not long enough to be a full project, but it's not just one visit. So instead of creating a new work order, we'd just add another site visit to that same work order. We also need to track time on install jobs even when there's no invoice that day. Like if we're putting a furnace in, we keep time on it even though we won't invoice until after.
>
> We also have maintenance checklists — like a checklist for technicians when they do maintenance on certain equipment. Right now those are PDFs. We'd want to be able to have the techs fill those out digitally.
>
> For the workflows, the implementation rep recommended we create a service workflow and an install workflow with different statuses depending on the job type. The work is pretty repetitive for some of our customers, so templates would help — same type of job with the same checklist items already attached.
