# ClearPath Test Scenario: Wayside Mechanical Ltd.

## Scenario Brief

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Company** | Wayside Mechanical Ltd. |
| **Industry** | Residential Plumbing / Mechanical |
| **Company Size** | Small — new division launching. Kevin (operations/dispatch), Kirt (owner/management), Danielle (tech), plus additional techs. Going live in ~25 days from this call. |
| **Job Types** | Service/repair calls (e.g., leaking hot water tank, leaking pipes). No separate estimate step — techs present good/better/best options directly on the invoice at the job site. |
| **Current Tools** | Brand new operation — no existing software. This is a greenfield setup. Coming from a parent company that likely uses other tools. |
| **Key Pain Points** | Need to be up and running by March 1. Techs need a simple, locked-down experience — Kevin doesn't want them moving things around or having too many options. Wants to control what techs see. Internal notes must stay private (e.g., "customer doesn't know where the main clean out is — it's in the northeast corner, move furniture to get to it"). Techs should present good/better/best repair options to the customer on-site. |
| **Dispatch Model** | Office-dispatched. Customer calls in → office creates customer → creates job → puts it in unassigned pool → assigns to a tech when ready. Tech gets a push notification. Jobs can be reassigned on the fly (tech calls in sick → reassign to another tech). |
| **Invoice Process** | No formal estimates. Tech arrives, diagnoses the issue, presents 3 options (good/better/best) directly as an invoice. On completion, tech writes a narrative description: "I arrived on site, was greeted at the door by Mary, took me to the hot water tank, found it leaking, presented three options, chose the middle option, did the work, Mary was walked through it and happy." Tech collects payment on site. |
| **Notes System** | Two types of notes needed: (1) Estimate/invoice notes visible to customer — the tech's narrative of what was done. (2) Internal notes invisible to customer — things like access instructions, property quirks, past visit details for future reference. |

## Customer Narrative

> We're Wayside Mechanical. We're a brand new division — we go live in about 25 days, so there's no existing workflow yet. We need to build this from scratch.
>
> Here's what I'm thinking the process looks like. A customer calls in. Someone in the office answers the phone, asks them some questions. They say "yeah, I need someone to come out." We create a customer, fill in all their information, and then create a job. The job goes into kind of a generic unassigned area. Then when we're ready, we pull it out and assign it to somebody.
>
> Once the job is assigned, the tech gets a notification. If Danielle calls in sick on Tuesday, I can just reassign it to Kevin. If the customer calls and wants to reschedule to Wednesday, I can move it. We just keep playing around with it until it's set.
>
> Now, when does the tech actually know they've got a job? Like what happens after I assign it? They need to get a notification — either through the app or email — so they know to go.
>
> We're not going to do formal estimates. What I want is for the tech to show up, take a look at things, and then present three options — good, better, best. Like "your hot water tank's toast, here are three options, pick whichever one is right for you." So the tech creates those options right there on the invoice. We skip the estimate step completely — our workflow is just create the customer, schedule the job, and go directly into invoicing.
>
> On the invoice, I want the tech to write up what happened. Like a narrative: "I arrived on site, was greeted at the door by Mary. She took me downstairs to the hot water tank. The hot water tank was leaking. I presented three options for repair, customer chose the middle option. We did that work. On completion, Mary was walked through it, she's happy with the work, no questions." That's what should be visible to the customer.
>
> But we also need internal notes that the customer never sees. Like "the main clean out is in the northeast corner of the building, you're going to have to move some furniture to get to it." Or "don't mention her silverware being all kinds of funny colors because that really gets her upset." Just stuff the tech needs to know for next time, but the customer shouldn't see.
>
> For the techs' mobile experience — the less they can set up the better. I don't want to give them options to move things around. I want all of their screens to look identical. They log in and it's right there — here's your job, here's what you need to do. Simple.
>
> We need to be fully operational by March first. Between now and then, we have to get through all the training, build this out, and start putting real jobs through it.
